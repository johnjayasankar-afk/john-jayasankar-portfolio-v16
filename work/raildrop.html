01

What was broken

Northeast Corridor trips are booked early for peace of mind, then the board keeps moving. Flexible tickets can be changed, but only if you notice the drop. Most people do not. They paid $128 and never learn Regional 95 is sitting at $47.

02

Product decision

Watch the corridor, not just the train you bought. Default window is travel day ±1. Compare against what you actually paid. Surface every eligible rail option. Alert once when the opportunity improves. Keep booking on Amtrak: copy the itinerary, confirm there, never invent a deep link or a fare.

03

Flow

  1. 01BookedYou already paid

    Peace of mind first. The board does not freeze for you.

  2. 02WatchSame stations, every train

    Regional, Acela, connections, not just your seat.

  3. 03AlertOne precise email

    Opportunity improved. Confirm on Amtrak before you change anything.

04

How it works

  • Book on Amtrak

    Whatever you actually paid is the number every train is measured against.

  • Tell RailDrop the trip

    Stations, date, and price. The window is searched immediately.

  • One email when it drops

    Confirm on Amtrak before you change anything.

No account required. Watch a trip as a guest, and add an email only if you want fare-drop alerts.

05

The watch discipline

  • Same stations, every train: Regional, Acela, and connections, not just the train you already bought
  • ±1 day by default: the day before, your travel day, and the day after. If tomorrow is cheaper than today, you should know
  • Checks immediately when a watch is created, then morning, afternoon, and evening; C rechecks on demand
  • Already booked a specific train? Add the train number and the board pins it next to the cheapest listed option
  • One precise alert: email only when the opportunity actually improves
06

Reading the board

  • Beats your train

    Cheaper and not slower than yours, or faster and not more expensive.

  • Filters that fit a trip

    Leave after, arrive by, a duration cap, and a 30-minute arrive buffer.

  • Change fees, only if you say so

    Type an estimate or tap $10, $20, or $50. Change rules depend on Flexible, Value, or Saver, so no fee is ever invented.

  • What moved, and how close

    The board shows what moved and how close departure is. The decision to change stays yours.

  • Built for the keyboard

    J and K focus a train, H hides it, U undoes, Y copies you versus it, W copies the window, F copies Amtrak fields.

  • Shareable in one line

    T copies stations, the cheapest listed train, and what you paid, for anyone else on the trip.

07

What it will not do

  • Invent Amtrak prices

    If the live board is down, you see that, not a guess.

  • Fake an itinerary

    No deep links into invented Amtrak itineraries. You copy trip details and book there.

  • Rebook for you

    It watches and ranks. You book on Amtrak, then tell it what you actually paid.

  • Spam you

    Alerts fire only when the opportunity actually improves.

08

Why I built it

  • I kept buying Flexible Amtrak tickets early, then learning too late that a later Regional was half the price
  • Existing tools either invent certainty or bury the signal in noise. I wanted quiet honesty
  • The product is the watch discipline: same stations, every rail option, alert only when it matters
09

How it was built

  • I directed the watch window, alert rule, and honesty constraints end-to-end
  • Implemented and iterated the live app in Cursor - product judgment and quality are mine
  • Alerts fire only when a listed fare beats what you paid; booking confirmation stays on Amtrak
  • A working launch, not a growth story. The case is about the discipline of the watch
10

Role & tags

Product and builder. Designed the watch window, honesty rules, alert discipline, and the live board that never pretends Amtrak is quieter than it is.

  • Fare watch
  • ±1 day board
  • No invented prices
  • You decide on Amtrak
11

Impact

A live product with immediate scans, scheduled rechecks, guest watches, optional email alerts, and a board that fails honestly when the source is down. Built end-to-end in Cursor. I made it after enough trips where the cheaper train was there the whole time. I just was not watching.
