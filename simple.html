how I build

Agents should earn autonomy. I start with the workflow, then give the system only the context, tools, and typed actions it needs, with a human gate on anything consequential. Model capability is one layer. Autonomy grows one rung at a time, on evidence. More on the control model.

Short theses for now. Longer notes land on Substack.

  • How much intelligence is this user action worth? · Routing intelligence is a unit-economics decision.
  • Autonomy is a product decision, not a model capability. · Evidence, reversibility, and control determine where an agent should be allowed to act.
  • The hardest AI products are often infrastructure products. · Data quality, APIs, entitlements, and workflow design decide whether the model actually ships.

independent products

Four independent products with case writeups, all shipped live and collected on Labs.

RideLens is every ride, one comparison. Live roads. Real rate cards. Uber, Lyft, Empower, and Curb ranked before you book. I built it end-to-end because I was tired of opening four apps and guessing. RideLens case writeup.
Daylight is adaptive display lighting for macOS: warmth and brightness on a schedule you set, entirely offline, with a printed six-layer precedence ladder and three levels of certainty about whether a change actually took. Daylight case writeup.
RailDrop watches Amtrak listed fares across your travel window and emails only when a qualifying option improves. It fails honestly when the source is down and never invents fees, fares, or fake itineraries. RailDrop case writeup.
Gridiron follows every live NFL and college football game on its own 3D field, with the reported ball spot, win probability, and odds from named sources. It draws only what a provider reported, and says so when something is missing. Gridiron case writeup.
